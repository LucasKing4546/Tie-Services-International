## The problem, as it usually gets described to us

**Cable out is a measurement, not a readout.** Layback depends on it, and so does
the position of every sounding line you have paid to acquire — but the number comes
off a counter that drifts, or a wheel that slips, or a cable that stretches under
load in a way nobody has accounted for. You find out in QC, three days after the
line was run.

Then there is how the cable behaves once it is in the water. Strum reaches the
sensor. Speed that will not hold steady at two metres a minute shows up as noise in
the record. A winch that cannot pay out smoothly at survey speed is not an
inconvenience — it is producing worse data than the sensor is capable of.

And the cable itself is the part that actually costs money. Armoured tow cable and
its termination are not cheap, and terminations fail at the join. Poor spooling
shortens cable life quietly, layer crushing layer, until the day it stops being
quiet.

Meanwhile the equipment has to move. Different hull next charter, different deck,
different seating, a lead-off angle that only works if the sheave sits somewhere the
vessel does not have. Every one of those is quay time nobody bills for.

None of it stops the campaign. That is the problem — it just erodes it.
