## One winch, five duties

Every family below shares the same engineering discipline — a Lebus grooved drum so the rope spools in a controlled path rather than crushing itself layer over layer, a level wind that places the rope instead of letting it land, and a brake margin stated honestly rather than to the edge of the standard. What changes between families is the duty: the cable, the working depth, the load, and how much of the deployment happens at slow, controlled speed rather than at line speed.

### The figure that actually matters

A winch's working load limit and line speed both change as the drum spools in — the wound radius grows with every layer, so line pull falls and line speed rises in the same proportion. A datasheet headline is usually the top-layer figure, which is the wrong one to size a deep or heavily loaded deployment against. Ask for the first-layer figure if that is where your work actually happens.

### Electric or hydraulic

Neither is the right answer for every deck. Electric drive is simpler to integrate on a vessel that already runs an electric bus and gives finer low-speed control. Hydraulic drive suits vessels with an existing hydraulic power unit and tends to handle sustained high-load duty with less thermal management. We size the drive to the deck and the duty rather than defaulting to one.
